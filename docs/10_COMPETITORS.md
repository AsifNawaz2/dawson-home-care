## How the franchise business model works (easy version)
1. **A national company owns the brand.** It builds the name, website, training programs, software, ads and "how to run an agency" playbook.
2. **A local person buys the right to run that brand in a territory** (e.g. "Hilton Head area"). They pay a one-time franchise fee to join, then keep paying the brand **a percentage of their sales every month** (royalty) plus a contribution to the national advertising fund.
3. **The local owner runs the office.** They rent an office, hire office staff (scheduler, care coordinator) and **hire caregivers as employees**. Each office is "independently owned and operated" — so quality varies a lot from office to office.
4. **How they make money:** families pay **by the hour** (often with a minimum visit, e.g. 3–4 hours). Most families pay out of pocket; some use long-term care insurance or VA benefits; a few programs use Medicaid waivers. The agency pays the caregiver an hourly wage; the difference pays for the office, insurance, training, recruiting, marketing and the franchise royalties.
5. **Their advantages:** a famous name, a professional national website, big ad budgets, branded training programs with trademarked names, awards from industry surveys (e.g. Home Care Pulse), lots of reviews.
6. **Their weaknesses (Dawson's openings):**
   - The owner is usually a business operator, not the person at your kitchen table → **Dawson: the owner visits every family personally.**
   - Many serve Beaufort from Bluffton/Hilton Head → **Dawson: headquartered in Beaufort.**
   - Corporate, templated websites and messaging → **Dawson: a warmer, more personal, more interactive site than any of them.**
   - Royalties push prices up and staff turnover is common → **Dawson: independent, family-run since 2002** (don't claim lower prices unless confirmed).
